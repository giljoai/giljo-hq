## Driving From Your Terminal

Everything in this guide can be done two ways: click through the dashboard, or tell your connected AI coding agent to do it for you. Both are first-class, permanent, and neither is the "real" way — pick whichever fits how you work in the moment, and switch any time.

The dashboard is where you fill things out, watch, and decide. The harness — your agent talking to GiljoAI over MCP — is the same lifecycle, driven headless. Whichever door does the work, the dashboard lights up live so you can always see it happen.

### Creating and Activating a Product

From the dashboard: use the product form (see **Products** above). From your terminal: ask your connected agent to create a product — the agent-driven doors on the welcome tour ("existing codebase," "shape an idea") do exactly this, writing the product and its vision document from a single prompt.

Activating a product is the same either way: click **Activate** on the Products page, or ask your agent to activate it. An agent-activated product looks and behaves identically to one you activated by hand.

### Staging and Launching a Project

The dashboard's **Stage Project → Staging… → Implement** flow (see **Projects → Staging a Project**) has a direct terminal equivalent: your agent stages the project itself, the **same server-enforced pause** waits for your explicit go-ahead before implementation starts — that pause is never agent politeness, it happens identically no matter which door is driving — and once you give the go, your agent launches implementation. Nothing about the pause, the phases, or closeout changes based on which door started the project.

### Chains

A chain's **conductor** (see **Chain Projects**) is always harness-driven — the dashboard cannot spawn agents, so an agent connected through your coding tool has to pick up a queued chain and run it. From your terminal, that same agent can also end a chain early or mark a finished member reviewed, without you opening the Projects page at all. The chain's group on the Jobs board keeps tracking the run either way.

### Multiple Products

If you have more than one product open in dashboard tabs, your agent can act on any product by name — it does not have to match whichever tab you happen to be looking at. Switching tabs never interrupts or redirects work your agent is doing on a different product.

### Why Both Doors Exist

Connecting a coding agent over MCP and then having to switch back to the browser to click Stage or Implement makes you a courier between your agent and the product. The harness door removes that: your agent can drive the whole lifecycle itself, start to finish, while the dashboard stays available whenever you want to look.
